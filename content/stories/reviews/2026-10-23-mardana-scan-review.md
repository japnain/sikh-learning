## Result and limits

All 16 content-page images listed below, plus the title/publication page, were opened and visually read, including printed page headers and relevant footnotes. The selected narrative details agree with the scanned text. Three source locator refinements follow: include p. 115 for the post-release lament's content; the Kartarpur settlement and clothing passage begins on printed p. 180; and the cremation variant is printed p. 182, footnote **1**. “388” is the Wikisource narrative's endnote number, not the number printed in the book.

These images verify what Macauliffe's later account says. They do not independently establish that its journeys, dialogue, wonders or final-rites narrative occurred as told. The pilot appropriately identifies the traditional character of these episodes and keeps its independent reviews pending.

The source is Max Arthur Macauliffe, *The Sikh Religion: Its Gurus, Sacred Writings and Authors*, vol. I (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1909), *Life of Guru Nanak*. The images inspected here came from Internet Archive item [`thesikhreligion01macauoft`](https://archive.org/details/thesikhreligion01macauoft). ## Specific unresolveds and recommended locator

1. The contextual Sultanpur/Nanak-from-Talwandi clauses are not independently established by p. 33 alone; they should be attributed to the surrounding life narrative rather than treated as statements found on that single leaf. P. 115's lament opening and the original 1909 title/publication leaf have now been visually checked.
2. Do not import the encyclopedia heading's 1459–1534 dates into the pilot from this review. No numerical birth/death date is in the inspected passages or the draft. The death-location narrative is verified as Macauliffe's Kartarpur/Ravi account, not adjudicated against other traditions.
3. The printed source itself states a religious-identity/conversion interpretation on p. 181 and discusses saloks on p. 182. The draft omits those contested claims. This image check does not resolve them or certify a conversion, salok authorship, instrument origin or independently established itinerary.
4. Independent English and Sikh history/Gurmat reviews remain pending. This review is a concrete page-image comparison only.

Recommended original-print locator: **vol. I, p. 33; pp. 44–45; pp. 80–81; pp. 101–102 and 105; pp. 111–115; pp. 180–182, especially p. 182 n. 1 (Wikisource endnote 388)**. Retain the distinction between a printed footnote and the website's endnote number.
