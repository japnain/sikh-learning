## Overall finding

The eight short readings generally work as introductory stories. They have identifiable people, consequential actions and economical handling of dialogue. Miraculous details are usually attributed at the point of narration as well as in the account notes. There is no editorial reason to inflate them to feature length.

The strongest recurring weakness is explaining an image or action again after the reader has understood it. This is mild in several short endings and more pronounced in the Mata Khivi pilot. The Khalsa and Mardana drafts gain depth from additional supported episodes; their improvement should come through sharper transitions and fewer abstract follow-up sentences.

Two changes are required for readiness within this AI English pass: K1 and X1 below. M1 is a suggested transition for clarity, not a factual or source error: the separate history reviewer reports that Macauliffe's Volume I, chapter IX explicitly describes another visit to Pak Pattan. Other changes are also suggestions, not findings that the original English is unacceptable. The illness wording in Amar Das is a sensitivity decision to resolve alongside the history/Gurmat review; it is already explicitly attributed to the tradition and should not be reported as an unattributed factual assertion.

## Per-story decisions and exact edits

### The Bread at Lalo's Table — `bhai-lalo`

**Verdict: English pass; optional tightening.** The meal, caste objection, feast and bread comparison form a complete arc. Lalo's response in p2 gives the opening a useful action. P8 attributes the milk and blood as traditional, and caste hierarchy is described without endorsing it.

No required English edits. Suggested B1: replace p9 with the following shorter ending. The current four-sentence explanation slightly repeats the distinction already made in p8.

> The carpenter's bread had been treated as beneath a distinguished guest. Beside the wealthy official's food, it revealed a different measure of worth: what had happened to other people before the meal reached the table.

Do not add an invented response or repentance scene for Malik Bhago to manufacture a stronger ending.

### The Water He Carried — `guru-amar-das-service`

**Verdict: English pass with a sensitivity wording decision for the history/Gurmat reviewer.** The repeated journey is concrete and gives the storm episode weight. P7 returns to utensils, wood and water without a separate moral. This is one of the collection's more effective endings.

No required English factual-attribution correction: p6 already begins with “The traditional account” and “It says”. The phrase “lost her reason”, however, is dated and can reduce the woman to an affliction. The traditional link between her condition and the rebuke also needs conscious specialist consideration; this AI English pass does not determine its religious meaning or recommend deleting the episode.

Suggested A1, conditional on the history/Gurmat reviewer agreeing that it preserves the source's claim: replace the second sentence of p6 with:

> It links the woman's mental illness to the rebuke.

Retain the attribution and subsequent recovery claim. Do not turn the traditional sequence into a narrator's general claim that mental illness results from disrespect.

Suggested A2: delete the last sentence of p2, “The account remembers the road between the two places as carefully as it remembers the work at either end.” The described journeys already establish the connection, and p7 makes the closing return to it.

### The Shade of a Shared Table — published `mata-khivi`

**Verdict: English pass as a short profile.** Its limited scope is clear. The text avoids invented kitchen orders, conversations and private thoughts. P3 properly describes the wider community's routine, and the account note states that it is not Khivi's documented personal timetable.

No required English edits. Suggested P1: in p5, replace the first two sentences with:

> The surviving passage is brief. It preserves no kitchen arrangements, list of workers or words Khivi spoke to the people she served.

This is a small rhythm edit. The existing version is acceptable. Keep the distinction between a brief early passage and later biographical context; the longer draft should replace this profile only through a deliberate editorial decision.

### The House That Hid Its Darkness — `sajjan`

**Verdict: English pass.** The narrative makes the concealed violence intelligible without dwelling on it. The hymn's imagery leads into recognition, confession and a concrete change in the use of stolen wealth. This preserves consequences beyond an emotional conversion.

No required or suggested prose edits. Preserve p8–p9's distinction between confession and action. Do not imply that distributing the property restores the victims' lives or constitutes a modern legal resolution. The text currently avoids that claim.

### The Milk and the Gold — `the-milk-and-the-coin`

**Verdict: English pass.** The villager's generosity, fear, return and regret make a coherent short episode. Both wonders are clearly identified as features of the telling. The ending lets the spiritual conversation continue beyond the material treasure rather than adding a separate moral.

No required English edits. Suggested G1: in p4, change “Wakefulness alone did not command it” to:

> Staying awake alone could not command that gift.

This makes the referent and argument easier to grasp on a first reading. Preserve the distinction between Shaikh Brahm and the earlier Shaikh Farid in the account note.

### A Needle for the Next World — `the-needle`

**Verdict: English pass; optional ending cut.** The wife has an active role, and the story does not manufacture a name or background for her. The needle remains an ordinary object, which makes the central challenge work. The omission of the preceding rebirth episode is disclosed.

No required English edits. Suggested N1: replace p9 with:

> The needle remained the small thing his wife had told him he could not take beyond death. By the end of the encounter, wealth was leaving Duni Chand's possession and reaching other people.

The current paragraph explains the same reversal through four sentences. This version keeps the object and ends on the resulting action.

### A Thread That Would Not Break — `the-sacred-thread`

**Verdict: English pass; optional tightening.** The images of cotton, strand, knot and strength give the narrative its structure. The text relates the challenge to conduct and exclusion without claiming to describe every Hindu practitioner. The notes correctly distinguish the verse anchor from the traditional ceremony setting.

No required English edits. Suggested T1: delete p2's last sentence, “The ceremony paused around a question about the very thing it had been assembled to confer.” The two questions already interrupt the scene; “assembled to confer” adds abstract phrasing without a new event.

Suggested T2: delete p7's third sentence, “When the old cord wore out, another could be provided, while the habits of the wearer remained unchanged.” P5 has already explained replacement of the cord, and p7's first two sentences make the conduct point. Either cut can be made independently.

### Water Turned West — `water-at-haridwar`

**Verdict: English pass; recommended local tightening.** The initial action is clear and memorable. The rosary and cooking-space exchanges are explicitly linked to the same source sequence. The extraordinary disclosure of thoughts is attributed where it occurs. The account note avoids extending this encounter's criticism to all Hindu devotion.

No required English edits. Suggested W1: replace p6 with:

> The listeners immediately objected. The field was far away; water thrown here could never reach it.

All four current sentences make the same objection. The shorter paragraph leaves room for the answering question to carry the scene.

### Becoming the Khalsa — draft `becoming-the-khalsa`

**Verdict: revise before passing this AI English review.** The strongest arc is the change from delegated intermediaries to the reciprocal initiation of the Guru and the five. The ordinary care in life-5 usefully broadens the ending beyond the public test. Distinguishing Sri Gur Sobha from Macauliffe is valuable and need not become a longer historiographical discussion.

**Required K1 — restore the narrative voice in five-3.** “The stories of the screened enclosure” introduces an enclosure that the body has not actually described; “This reading does not reconstruct…” then addresses editorial method at the scene's central moment. The editorial note already records this omission. Replace the whole paragraph with:

> In Macauliffe's version, the five emerged alive and were brought before the people. The men who had offered themselves now stood beside the Guru. The gathering saw the courage of people who had been sitting among them.

This keeps the particular source visible and follows the offer-of-life sequence without inventing action inside the enclosure. It leaves the selection rationale in the existing note. The history reviewer should resolve any more substantive concern about omitting the traditional enclosure sequence; the English fix alone does not settle that question.

Suggested K2 — replace the last sentence of bowl-3, “Readiness to use arms was joined to a duty of protection; strength had people to answer for beyond the person who possessed it”, with:

> Those who bore arms were also responsible for protecting others.

The current phrase “strength had people to answer for” is difficult to parse and repeats the preceding duty in abstract form.

Suggested K3 — replace bowl-5's second through fourth sentences with:

> The Guru accepted the same discipline he had given them.

Retain its opening “The five did so” and its final attributed sentence. Three successive restatements of the reversal weaken an action that is already striking.

Suggested K4 — remove the final sentence of bowl-6, “The shared identity required acts that made such recognition possible.” The section already names initiation, teaching and recognition; the sentence does not add a concrete consequence.

### The Music Beside the Journey — draft `bhai-mardana`

**Verdict: English pass with a suggested transition and optional tightening.** Mardana is a participant who asks questions, needs food, makes choices and plays music, rather than a name repeatedly inserted beside the Guru. The horse-and-instrument difficulty makes the captivity scene tangible. The final-rites variant is explicit without overwhelming the farewell.

**Suggested M1 — make the episode transition neutral.** The start of a-song-heard, “On a later visit to Pak Pattan”, is source-supported: the separate history reviewer checked Macauliffe's Volume I, chapter IX, which says “again” and describes another visit. My initial unsupported-chronology finding was therefore too strong and is withdrawn. A neutral transition can still help distinguish the source's sequence from an independently established itinerary, particularly because the account note already makes that distinction. Suggested replacement for the first two sentences:

> In another traditional episode, the two men rested outside Pak Pattan. Shaikh Kamal, a disciple of Shaikh Brahm, was gathering firewood nearby.

The remaining paragraph can continue unchanged. This wording emphasizes the editorial arrangement of traditional episodes without rejecting Macauliffe's repeat-visit statement or establishing an independent chronology.

Suggested M2 — delete the last sentence of the-conversation, “A passer-by had listened, asked for a repetition, remembered and returned with the song.” Carried-home has already told that sequence clearly. The repetition delays the move into captivity.

Suggested M3 — replace the-question's first two sentences, “He said so. The account gives him the question that the departure has left unresolved”, with:

> Mardana asked why.

The preceding paragraph has already established his confusion. His question can proceed directly to the Guru's answer.

Do not lengthen the death section with invented bereavement, interior thoughts or a harmonized account of the rites. The existing variant is part of its editorial honesty.

### The Shade of a Shared Table — draft `mata-khivi`

**Verdict: revise for repetition before passing this AI English review.** The direct Ang 967 anchor, the household's outward hospitality and the independent part played by Bibi Amro give this version useful substance. Its extra length should come from these distinctions. It currently returns too often to the same food/resources/community interpretation, especially p2–p4 and p8–p10.

**Required X1 — delete p4 in full.** It repeats p2's placement of generosity within sacred song and p3's explanation of food reaching the community. It introduces no new action or biographical detail. The next section begins at p5, so no section start needs to be reassigned. Retain the existing IDs for all surviving paragraphs.

Suggested X2 — replace p8 with:

> When Macauliffe describes Guru Angad's community at Khadur, offerings are directed towards the kitchen and the support of travellers and pilgrims.

The three following current sentences repeat this circulation of gifts. P9 then supplies the concrete daily routine.

Suggested X3 — replace p10 with:

> The older verse connects Khivi with the distribution of food. It remembers the resources and care made available through her work.

This removes the obvious generalization “A shared meal has to reach the people for whom it is prepared” and the additional restatement of its setting. Whether “leadership” is the right historical inference from the verse remains a history/Gurmat decision, not an English certification.

Retain the Amro passage as her own contribution. Do not imply that the sources document Khivi teaching Amro the particular hymn or directing every activity in the community. Keep the bowl-of-rice ending: it returns to the opening image economically.
