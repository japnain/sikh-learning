Checked 23 October 2026 by an AI scan-review agent against reading revision 2 of `content/stories/drafts/becoming-the-khalsa.json` (1,452 words). This is a source-image comparison, not a human Sikh history/Gurmat review or authentication of the events reported by a later author.

## Witness and method

Witness: Max Arthur Macauliffe, *The Sikh Religion: Its Gurus, Sacred Writings and Authors*, volume V, original 1909 edition, University of Toronto/Robarts copy. [Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/thesikhreligioni05macauoft); [reader at printed p. 84](https://archive.org/details/thesikhreligioni05macauoft/page/84/mode/2up).

All **15 printed pages, 84–98 inclusive**, were opened as actual page images with the image viewer and visually read, including their visible page numbers and chapter headings. OCR was not counted as image inspection. Pages 99–102 and the title/imprint leaves were **not visually inspected in this pass**. The draft's narrative claims did not require extending beyond p. 98; its bibliographic publication statement is outside the pages audited here.

The parent implementation agent acquired the JPEGs from Internet Archive's individual-page route. This agent inspected those local images. The reproducibility manifest at `tmp/story-source-scans/acquired/manifest.json` records each original URL, leaf number and SHA-256. Local files are in `tmp/story-source-scans/acquired/v5/`, named `p84-n95.jpg` through `p98-n109.jpg`. These are private research intermediates, not public story assets.

For this range, **zero-based scan leaf = printed page + 11**. The correspondence was verified from the printed number visible on every inspected image, not inferred only from an OCR index.

## Disposition

**No factual story-prose correction is required by this visual comparison.** The main improvements are precise locations and explicit normalization records. All Macauliffe narrative paragraphs have support within the audited range, with interpretive limits noted above. Pages 99–102 are not needed to support the selected narrative and are not claimed as visually checked.

The edition's goats, sparrows, crowd figures, named woman adding sweets and colonial-era footnotes remain visible source material; their selective omission does not establish their historical truth or falsity. Scan comparison neither clears the modern *Sri Gur Sobha* translation for republication nor completes the pending independent human history/Gurmat and English reviews. No JSON, dossier or public catalog entry was changed by this agent.
