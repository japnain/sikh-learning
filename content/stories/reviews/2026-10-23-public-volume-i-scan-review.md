# Public Guru Nanak readings: Volume I page-image review

Date: 23 October 2026. Review type: implementation-agent comparison of public reading claims with displayed source page images. This is not an independent English editorial review, Sikh history/Gurmat review, human certification, or verification that the narrated events occurred.

## Material and method

The six public readings and their research dossiers were read: `bhai-lalo`, `the-sacred-thread`, `sajjan`, `water-at-haridwar`, `the-needle`, and `the-milk-and-the-coin`. All six cite Max Arthur Macauliffe, *The Sikh Religion: Its Gurus, Sacred Writings and Authors*, original 1909 Oxford edition, Volume I.

The parent implementation agent acquired the Internet Archive page images. This reviewing agent then opened and actually viewed all 22 relevant images listed below with the image-viewing tool, reading the printed page headers and relevant narrative, verse, and footnotes. These conclusions therefore do not rest only on OCR, Wikisource transcription, filenames, or archive metadata. The locally acquired JPEGs are under `tmp/story-source-scans/acquired/v1/`; `tmp/story-source-scans/acquired/manifest.json` records their acquisition URLs and SHA-256 hashes. The scans were used for checking, not added to published story assets.

The exact Internet Archive item used is [`thesikhreligion01macauoft`](https://archive.org/details/thesikhreligion01macauoft). Note the spelling: there is no extra `i` before `01`. Internet Archive's `n` index is zero-based. For the inspected pages, `n = printed page + 95`; the corresponding one-based Wikisource DjVu leaf is `printed page + 96`. The visible printed headers confirmed the mapping individually.

## Claim checks

### `bhai-lalo`: printed pp.43–44, chapter IV

| Public paragraphs | Visually checked support | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| p1–p2 | p.43: arrival at Saiyidpur with Mardana, shelter with Lalo the carpenter, request to eat within sacred lines, truth/purity response and meal at the existing seat. | Supported. The reading's explanation of a ritual boundary is an indirect summary. |
| p3 | p.43: wish to leave after two days, Lalo's persuasion to remain, criticism of a Khatri's son lodging with a Sudar. | Supported; the caste order is the account's challenged social setting. |
| p4 | p.43: Malik Bhago identified as steward, a feast for Hindus of all four castes, Brahman messenger, Guru's answer and warning of displeasure. | Supported. Calling Bhago a powerful official is a reasonable compression of steward to the owner of Saiyidpur. |
| p5–p7 | p.44: absence, summons, faqir/food response, renewed caste objection, request for both portions and squeezing of bread. | Supported. The reading omits the source's right-hand/left-hand detail without changing the action. |
| p8 | p.44: milk from Lalo's bread and blood from Bhago's, explicitly introduced as something said; explanation contrasts honest labour with bribery and oppression. | Supported as a miraculous element of the narrated tradition. The retelling correctly attributes the explanatory interpretation to Macauliffe. |
| p9 | Interpretation of p.44. | Editorial synthesis, not an extra event or dialogue. |

No prose correction is required by the inspected source. Preserve the traditional-account qualification.

The public locator should read **Chapter VII, pp.84–91**, distinguishing the setting/identity and opening exchange on pp.84–87 from the milk/basin episode on pp.88–91. The dossier should map p1 to pp.84–88 and p2–p4 specifically to p.88. The first contextual Wikisource image is [leaf 180](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:The_Sikh_Religion,_its_gurus,_sacred_writings_and_authors_Vol_1.djvu/180); the narrative reading link may start at [printed p.84](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sikh_Religion/Volume_1/Life_of_Guru_Nanak#84).
