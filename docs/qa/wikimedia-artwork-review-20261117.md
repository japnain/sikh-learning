All four fresh original downloads returned HTTP 429. No downloaded bytes are claimed as inspected. Three identities were instead verified against the original-file SHA-1 recorded in the provider's structured file metadata. The register independently retains SHA-256 fingerprints of every local file. SHA-1 here establishes catalogue correspondence, not authenticity against a malicious collision.

[Creative Commons' PDM explanation](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/) supports copying, adaptation and distribution of identified public-domain works, while excluding warranties and noting jurisdictional differences. Approval above records the bounded evidence decision, not a guarantee for every jurisdiction or every use.

[V&A website terms](https://www.vam.ac.uk/info/va-websites-terms-conditions), marked September 2026, distinguish permitted noncommercial collection uses from homepage uses. Their stated free-use conditions include a 768-pixel digital limit, time limits and a museum credit; homepage use is directed to separate licensing. The two V&A reproductions are larger and used on Home. This is an unresolved conflict with Commons' public-domain assessment, not a conclusion that the museum necessarily owns copyright in faithful reproductions or that NaamRas infringes. No permission request was sent.

Result: two of 108 register entries approved, **106 unresolved/blocked**. Rights inventory still passes; strict release gate still fails. Existing source credits and all personal data remain unchanged.
