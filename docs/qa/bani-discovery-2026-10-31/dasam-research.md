## 4: Jaap Sahib

Guru Gobind Singh Ji’s hymn of praise addresses the Divine beyond form, caste, birth and death through a succession of names and attributes.

Its 199 stanzas are recited in morning Nitnem and during Amrit Sanchar, the Sikh initiation ceremony.

Evidence:

- Provider opening heading identifies Patshahi 10; initial verse describes the Divine without form/caste; final stanza is numbered 199.
- SGPC Sikh Rehat Maryada identifies Jaap for daily morning recitation and Amrit Sanchar.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/4)
- [SGPC: Sikh Rehat Maryada](https://sgpc.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Sikh-Rehat-Maryada-English-n.pdf)
- [SGPC: Guru Gobind Singh Ji and his writings](https://sgpc.net/ten-guru-sahibs/guru-gobind-singh-sahib/)

## 5: Shabad Hazare (Patshahi 10)

Ten hymns of Guru Gobind Singh Ji explore devotion to the One, inner discipline and longing for the Beloved.

This collection includes the shabad Mitar Pyare Nu and is distinct from the Guru Granth Sahib selection also called Shabad Hazare.

Evidence:

- Provider selection contains ten raag/khyal headings and ends with stanza sequence marker 10.
- SikhRI identifies a collection of ten shabads by the Tenth Guru, distinguishes the SGGS selection of the same name, and discusses devotion, inner discipline and the Beloved Friend shabad.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/5)
- [Sikh Research Institute: Sabad Hajare Patisahi 10](https://sikhri.org/tags/sabad-hajare)

## 6: Tav Prasad Savaiye (Sraavag Suddh)

These ten savaiye from Akal Ustat question reliance on ritual, wealth and worldly power, and affirm loving devotion to the Divine.

Beginning Sraavag Suddh, this is the morning Nitnem selection, numbered 21–30 within Akal Ustat; the Dheenan Ki selection contains different verses.

Evidence:

- Provider has ten numbered stanzas 21–30 beginning Sraavag Suddh, with love explicitly central in stanza 29.
- The SGPC code specifies this opening for the ten savaiye in morning Nitnem and Amrit Sanchar.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/6)
- [SGPC: Sikh Rehat Maryada](https://sgpc.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Sikh-Rehat-Maryada-English-n.pdf)

## 7: Tav Prasad Savaiye (Dheenan Ki)

A separate sequence of ten savaiye from Akal Ustat begins by praising the One who sustains the humble and all living beings.

These are stanzas 243–252 of Akal Ustat, distinct from the Sraavag Suddh sequence; they turn attention from outward observances toward remembrance of the Timeless One.

Evidence:

- Provider has ten numbered stanzas 243–252 beginning Dheenan Ki; content praises sustaining all beings and contrasts ritual observances with remembrance.
- This is a different excerpt of Akal Ustat, not an alternate spelling or exact duplicate of ID 6.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/7)
- [iGurbani: Savaiye Deenan](https://www.igurbani.com/read/3-svaiye-deenan)

## 8: Akal Ustat Chaupai

The opening chaupai section of Akal Ustat praises the One Creator present throughout creation and beyond distinctions of form and caste.

This selection contains the opening invocations and ten chaupai stanzas of the longer Akal Ustat composition.

Evidence:

- Provider includes the opening invocations and chaupai stanzas 1–10; the same opening is present within the longer ID 29.
- Containment does not make this exact selection a duplicate of the longer composition.

Sources:

## 9: Benti Chaupai Sahib

Guru Gobind Singh Ji’s prayer of supplication asks for protection, guidance and steadfast devotion to the One.

It is recited within Rehras Sahib and during Amrit Sanchar; different recitation traditions include different opening and closing passages.

Evidence:

- Provider title is Benti Chaupai; its supplied long text includes preceding passages at 1385 and concluding Savaiya/Dohra at page 290.
- SGPC Rehat Maryada places Benti Chaupai in Rehras and Amrit Sanchar with specified start/end words; provider recitation-length flags preserve different traditions.
- Do not equate one length setting or the entire combined selection with a universal text extent.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/9)
- [SGPC: Sikh Rehat Maryada](https://sgpc.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Sikh-Rehat-Maryada-English-n.pdf)

## 12: Chandi Charitar Ustat

The six-stanza closing section of the second Chandi Charitar moves from the aftermath of battle to verses of praise.

Also headed Ath Chandi Charitar Ustat, this is the eighth chapter, numbered 257–262, rather than the entire Chandi Charitar narrative.

Evidence:

- Actual provider has 26 lines, begins Ath Chandi Charitra Ustat Barnanam, and contains six stanzas numbered 1/257 through 6/262.
- Closing colophon explicitly calls this the eighth chapter. Punjabi University Encyclopaedia identifies the second Chandi Charitra as eight cantos and 262 verses.
- Therefore this selection is the concluding Ustat chapter, not all of Chandi Charitra I or II. iGurbani independently reproduces the same six stanzas.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/12)
- [iGurbani: Chandi Charitar Ustat](https://www.igurbani.com/read/9-chandi-charitar-ustat)
- [Punjabi University, Encyclopaedia of Sikhism: Chandi Charitra](https://eos.learnpunjabi.org/CHANDI%20CHARITRA.html)

## 13: Chandi Di Vaar (Vaar Sri Bhagauti Ji Ki)

This Punjabi martial ballad recounts Chandi’s battles against demonic forces through vivid scenes of conflict and courage.

Its opening pauri, beginning Pritham Bhagauti Simar Kai, forms the opening of Ardaas; the full Vaar continues through 55 pauris.

Evidence:

- Actual provider sequence runs through pauri 55 and ends with Durga Ki Vaar colophon.
- SGPC biography describes the Chandi martial narrative; SGPC Ardaas text begins with the Vaar heading and first pauri.
- Keep this full ballad distinct from both Ardaas and the six-stanza Chandi Charitra excerpt.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/13)
- [SGPC: Guru Gobind Singh Ji and his writings](https://sgpc.net/ten-guru-sahibs/guru-gobind-singh-sahib/)
- [SGPC: Ardaas](https://sgpc.net/ardas/)

## 19: Shastar Naam Mala (Opening Chapter)

The opening chapter of Shastar Naam Mala invokes protection through the names and imagery of swords, arrows and other weapons.

This selection is the 27-couplet Bhagauti Ustat chapter; the much longer Shastar Naam Mala continues beyond it.

Evidence:

- Actual provider has 61 lines on pages 717–718, ends at couplet 27 and has an explicit first-chapter Bhagauti Ustat completion colophon.
- SGPC Gurdwara Gazette November 2020 describes the much longer Shastar Nam Mala, while iGurbani reproduces this first chapter.
- Existing catalog range 717–809 is the parent work, not the selected BaniDB text; discovery title must identify the opening chapter.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/19)
- [iGurbani: Shastar Naam Mala Bhagauti Ustat](https://www.igurbani.com/read/9-shastar-naam-mala-bhaugati-ustat)

## 24: Ardaas (Prayer of Supplication)

The Sikh communal prayer remembers the Gurus, the Panth’s sacrifices and shared commitments, and asks for guidance and the welfare of all.

Its opening comes from Vaar Sri Bhagauti Ji Ki; the prayer continues with remembrance of Sikh history and supplications for the community.

Evidence:

- SGPC publishes Ardaas with Vaar Sri Bhagauti Ji Ki opening, remembrance of the Gurus and Sikh sacrifices, and prayer for the good of all.
- The whole later communal prayer should not be attributed solely to Guru Gobind Singh Ji because its opening bears Patshahi 10.

Sources:

- [SGPC: Ardaas](https://sgpc.net/ardas/)
- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/24)

## 25: Sri Bhagauti Astotr (Panth Prakash)

The Panth Prakash version of this praise of Bhagauti centres on the sword, martial courage and protection.

Its wording and additional closing chaupai distinguish it from the Hazur Sahib version.

Evidence:

- Actual provider label specifies Panth Prakash; 54 lines include different wording from ID 26 and an additional final Chaupai after the shared closing appeal.
- This is a distinct recension. Do not delete or merge as an exact duplicate of ID 26.
- Provider page 1428 is not treated as reliable evidence of a universal Dasam Granth pagination or manuscript placement.

## 26: Sri Bhagauti Astotr (Hazur Sahib)

The Hazur Sahib version of this praise of Bhagauti invokes the sword through images of strength, courage and protection.

This version has its own wording and closes with an appeal to protect Guru Gobind Singh, without the additional chaupai in the Panth Prakash version.

Evidence:

- Actual provider label specifies Sri Hazur Sahib; 40 lines end with the appeal for Guru Shah Gobind’s protection.
- Opening body wording differs from ID 25 and the final additional Chaupai is absent; retain both clearly named versions.

## 28: Barah Maha Savaiye (Krishna Avtar)

This twelve-month sequence from Krishna Avtar follows the gopis’ memories of Krishna and their longing as the seasons change.

Beginning with Phagan, it comprises stanzas 867–879 and is distinct from the Barah Maha compositions in Guru Granth Sahib.

Evidence:

- Actual provider has 54 lines with Barah Maha/Savaiya headings, begins Phagun Mai, and spans stanzas 867–879 at pages 383–384.
- SikhRI Guru Granth Sahib Project identifies this first Krishna Avtar Barah Maha as 867–879 and a second sequence as 911–925.
- This selection is the Phagan sequence, not the separate Prem Chhaki sequence or either SGGS Barah Maha.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/28)
- [The Guru Granth Sahib Project: Barah Maha as a poetic form](https://gurugranthsahib.io/bani/introduction/22/Introduction)

## 29: Akal Ustat (Praise of the Timeless One)

Guru Gobind Singh Ji’s extended praise of the Timeless One affirms the Divine beyond religious and social divisions.

The opening chaupai and both Tav Prasad Savaiye selections are passages within this longer composition.

Evidence:

- Provider longer selection has 1091 lines, opening chaupai and both savaiye sequences, and ends after stanza 271 with Ustat Sampooran.
- SGPC biography and Gazette describe praise of the Divine and rejection of narrow social/religious exclusivity.
- Do not label this selection as just another copy of the opening Chaupai or either ten-savaiye excerpt; do not claim manuscript completeness.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/29)
- [SGPC: Guru Gobind Singh Ji and his writings](https://sgpc.net/ten-guru-sahibs/guru-gobind-singh-sahib/)
- [SGPC Gurdwara Gazette: Akal Ustat, page 194](https://sgpc.net/gazette/2020/November/Nov-English.pdf)

## 53: Ugardanti

A six-part composition invokes Bhagauti through images of divine power, battle and protection, with supplications for the Khalsa Panth.

Also called Bhagauti Chhand Chhakka, its language draws on the imagery of Durga and includes appeals for compassion and devotion.

Evidence:

- Actual provider title is Ugardanti; heading calls it Chhand Chhakka Bhagauti Ju Ka and enumerates chhands two through six, ending with a six-chhand colophon.
- Primary text invokes Bhagauti/Durga imagery, requests compassion/devotion and includes explicit Khalsa Panth references.
- Only content-based description used. No claim about uncontested authorship, universal canonical placement, miraculous benefits or fixed standard pagination.

Sources:

- [BaniDB: selected text and headings](https://api.banidb.com/v2/banis/53)
- [NaamSimran: Ugardanti text and translation](https://www.namsimran.com/banis/53)

## zafarnama: Zafarnama (Letter of Victory)

Guru Gobind Singh Ji’s Persian verse letter to Emperor Aurangzeb challenges broken promises and asserts moral responsibility in the exercise of power.

Written at Dina after the evacuation of Anandpur, it condemns the breach of sworn assurances of safe passage.

Evidence:

- SGPC biography explicitly identifies a Persian verse letter written at Dina to Emperor Aurangzeb condemning broken sworn assurances after the evacuation of Anandpur and asserting morality in political conduct.
- Exact battle/event dates are intentionally omitted because dates vary across historical accounts.

Sources:

- [SGPC: Guru Gobind Singh Ji and his writings](https://sgpc.net/ten-guru-sahibs/guru-gobind-singh-sahib/)
